The Man Who Dared to Dream

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on October 15, 1931, in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. From the humble home of a boat owner, he rose to become the 11th President of India — a journey powered not by privilege, but by relentless curiosity, discipline, and an unshakable belief in the power of dreams.

He was a man of paradoxes — a nuclear scientist who loved poetry, a defence technologist who taught children, a President who ate simple meals and wore the same worn sandals. India called him the People's President for good reason.

A Life in Milestones

1931

Born in Rameswaram

Born to a boat-owner father and a devout mother in a small island town in Tamil Nadu, Kalam's childhood was marked by curiosity and simplicity.

1958

Joins DRDO

After graduating from MIT Chennai, he began his scientific career at the Defence Research and Development Organisation, setting the foundation for India's aerospace dreams.

1969

ISRO — SLV Project

Transferred to ISRO, Kalam became the project director of India's first indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III), successfully deploying the Rohini satellite.

1983

Missile Man of India

Led the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP), birthing missiles like Agni, Prithvi, Akash, and Trishul — making India a formidable power.

1998

Pokhran-II Nuclear Tests

Played a pivotal role in India's successful nuclear tests, establishing India as a declared nuclear power on the world stage.

2002

11th President of India

Elected as the President of India with overwhelming cross-party support, becoming one of the most beloved leaders the country has ever known.

2015

Eternal Farewell

On July 27, 2015, while delivering a lecture to students at IIM Shillong — doing what he loved most — his heart gave way. India lost its brightest star.

A Legacy That Ignites

Beyond the missiles and the medals, Kalam's true legacy is in the hearts of millions of students he inspired. He believed that India's strength lay in its youth, and he spent every moment after his presidency visiting schools, colleges, and universities — talking not at young people, but with them.

He was awarded the Bharat Ratna in 1997, India's highest civilian honour. But he once said that the greatest honour was a child calling him teacher.

His book Wings of Fire, an autobiography, has inspired countless readers worldwide and remains one of the most powerful accounts of perseverance, purpose, and patriotism ever written.
